## What the flow does, and why it is shaped that way

Nine items — three brands × three lengths — and **only the first is built from scratch.** The other
eight are BCP's own **Duplicate**, renamed, with the one dropdown that differs re-picked, then
Update.

⚠ **Duplicate does NOT save what is in the form.** The order is duplicate → edit → Update, never
edit → duplicate. Tested on the live page: a marker typed into the name field without pressing
Update appeared in neither the original row nor the clone. The page submits an empty body and the
server copies the committed row. Worth knowing if the footage ever looks like the edit came first.

## The words

21 scenes, **20 spoken and 1 silent**, 451 words.

⚠ **Scene 18 is silent on purpose, and it is 156.2 seconds long.** Carson, 2026-09-10: "add the
voice for every scene, except for the the items, since they are all repeats of the same first
item." So the eight clones are a **single** scene with no cue, exactly as long as the footage it
covers. The first item's closing line carries it — *"That is the first variant saved. Every other
one is a copy of it with just one dropdown changed."*

**An editor should trim or speed that stretch.** It is repeats, nothing is said over it, and the
`.vtt` says so in the scene's NOTE rather than leaving an empty cue that reads as a missing line.

## Nothing was stretched

0 screens short, which is why `stretch_report.json` says `stretched_any: false`. The capture was
recorded at a flat 7000ms minimum first, its screens measured off the delivered mp4, then the lines
written to fit what was already there — the order `Screens-to-Scenes.txt` asks for. No frame was
duplicated, so `built` in that report is the real screen length and can be trusted as screen edges.

## Known caveat

⚠ **`estimated-word-time` in `script.json` is an estimate, and it is wrong in both directions.** It
is `words / 3.44` and has no term for punctuation, but `say` pauses at every comma and dash.
Measured here: 21 words with four commas took 8.18s where the formula said 7.9s. **Use
`narration_report.json`** — it holds the measured length of every line. Every line in this folder
was checked against its screen with the real voice, so none is rushed; all play at 155 wpm.
